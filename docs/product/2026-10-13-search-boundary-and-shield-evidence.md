## Public-API separation checked again on13Oct

Apple describes `WebContentSettings.FilterPolicy.auto` as its adult-content filtering policy. Separately, `ShieldSettings.webDomains` accepts selected opaque website tokens and documents callbacks to appearance/action extensions, with a50-token limit. These contracts are distinct; configuring adult auto-filtering does not establish those selected-shield callbacks for every adult destination. The observed selected example.com generic shield still fails and needs its own explanation. [Adult filtering](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/managedsettings/webcontentsettings/filterpolicy/auto%28_%3Aexcept%3A%29), [selected website shields](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/managedsettings/shieldsettings/webdomains-swift.property).

Safari’s declarative redirect contract requires host access and `declarativeNetRequestWithHostAccess`; supported rules can target a bundled extension page. Candidate17 preparation explicitly packages only the already-reviewed safe fixtures and their payload-free OpenFREED entry. Domain-page redirect while DNS remains active, permissions, app access and challenge must be separately tested. This does not operate as an installed Safari extension in every other browser. [Apple DNR documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/safariservices/blocking-content-with-your-safari-web-extension), [Apple’s current walkthrough](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/216/).
